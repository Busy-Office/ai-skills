The draft

Intent — acme-export

For a data analyst at a 10–50 person company, who today files a ticket and waits two days for an engineer to pull numbers they could read themselves.

The change: they answer a new question — and hand the result to someone else — without involving an engineer at all.

The bet: the blocker is export and sharing, not querying; teams already have a warehouse they can query and no way to get an answer out of it.

We will know it worked when the median new question goes from asked to answered in under 10 minutes, on 20 live accounts, by the end of Q3.
It was wrong if analysts still route new questions through an engineer after export ships — then the blocker was querying, not sharing.

Non-goals: we are not building a BI suite with its own chart language; and we are not building scheduled reporting this year — tempting, asked for twice, and it turns the product into a pipeline tool.

Objectives — this horizon

  1. An analyst gets an answer out of the tool without help — measure: export completes for 95% of queries under 30 s
  2. A result is shareable with someone who has no account — measure: 1 in 3 exports is opened by a second person
  3. A new account reaches its first answer the day it signs up — measure: median time-to-first-export under 24 h
Key focus — now: objective 1, getting an answer out. Until 20 accounts have exported twice. Objectives 2 and 3 wait, on purpose.
TO DECIDE — is the audience still one analyst, or the team that bought SSO in March? The answer changes objective 3 and the rank of four backlog items.

To decide

  1. Who first — the analyst, or the team? Four items and objective 3 are blocked on this.
  2. Which is proof: time-to-answer, or exports opened by a second person? Both are in the sources; only one can be the headline measure.
  3. When export and scheduled reporting compete next month, which waits? The answer is the non-goal that costs something.
